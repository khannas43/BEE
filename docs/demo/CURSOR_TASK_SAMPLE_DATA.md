## Hard rules
1. **Idempotent.** Running `npm run demo:seed` twice must change nothing the second time (`ON CONFLICT ... DO NOTHING/UPDATE`, fixed UUIDs in the same style as the existing files, no `random()`/`now()`-dependent keys; relative dates such as `now() - interval '5 days'` are fine for display times but keys must be fixed).
2. **Do not break the presenter's path.** These records must stay exactly as they are because the script and tests use them: DEMO-PAY-0001..0005 (0001 must still have no payment), certificates `BEE/RAC/2026/10101` to `10104` (not submitted to the ledger), `10901`, `10902`, the QR serials `BEEQ-S2D0000001` to `25`, `BEEQ-S2D0000030`, `BEEQ-S2D0000040`, the 2025 ledger certificates `90037` (seeded MISMATCH), `90038` (failed), `90039` (pending), `90040` (active), production quarters 2025-Q4 to 2026-Q3 for Nova, the users and passwords. Add around them; never rename or delete them.
3. **Append-only and audit tables** (`payment_event`, `qr_event`, `ledger_event`, `production_*_event`, `ai_*` runs) need matching rows written in the same style the existing seed files use (see how s3_ledger writes `ledger_event`). A hash chain in `ledger_tx` must be valid: reuse the hash function/approach already in `s3_ledger.sql`; only the single labelled mismatch certificate may fail verification.
4. **Synthetic and plausible.** Brand/organisation names obviously fictional but realistic (for example "Aurora Cooling Pvt Ltd (synthetic)"); star ratings 1 to 5, realistic ISEER/units/capacities for room air conditioners and refrigerators; dates inside 2025-11 to 2026-10; amounts consistent with the fee rule already in the data (INR 10,000 plus 18% tax per application unless the existing seed says otherwise).
5. **Consistency across screens.** The same organisation/model/certificate should tell one story everywhere: an application that is approved has a fee payment, a certificate, QR serials, ledger status and production rows that agree with each other, and the MIS totals (which are computed live from these tables) should come out as believable round-ish numbers.

## What "proper" means per screen (targets, adjust to the data model)
- **MIS dashboard** (`s5_mis.sql`): about 120 model applications over the last 12 months across 8 synthetic organisations and 3 product categories (room AC, refrigerator, ceiling fan if the master data allows it; otherwise RAC only), with a realistic funnel (draft, fee due, IAME scrutiny, BEE scrutiny, rating, director review, approved, returned, rejected), a visible seasonal shape (more filings before summer), average approval time 8 to 15 days, fees collected in the INR 20 to 40 lakh range, a few open delegations. Charts must look like real trends, not a flat line.
- **Payments** (`s1_payments.sql`): 15 to 20 payments across 6 organisations in all four states (initiated, paid, failed, paid with receipt), spread over 12 months, plus about 5 paid-but-not-yet-confirmed and 2 confirmed differences so the reconciliation panel has something to show; keep five fee-due applications for the live demo.
- **QR batches and serials** (`s2_qr.sql`): 8 to 10 batches across organisations with believable quantities (500 to 5,000), most partly allocated, some fully bound, a couple closed, a handful of void serials, so the list and the batch detail look busy.
- **Certificate ledger** (`s3_ledger.sql`): about 30 certificates in the ledger in varied states (active majority, a few pending, 2 failed with retries, 2 amended, 2 revoked), each with a valid hash chain, plus the four existing special ones. Block explorer should read like a real feed (varied times, several kinds: issue, amend, revoke).
- **Quarterly production** (`s4_production.sql`): 5 organisations with 4 quarters each (2025-Q4 to 2026-Q3) in states accepted/submitted/query, 6 to 12 model rows per submission with realistic unit counts, CA evidence rows, and 2 or 3 deliberate over-declaration cases that the reconciliation flags against bound QR serials (keep 2026-Q3 Nova's spike).
- **AI use cases** (`s6_ai.sql`): the five tools need richer inputs so their output looks convincing: compliance-risk needs entities with varied histories (high, medium, low risk); production-anomaly needs several monthly series with 2 or 3 real spikes; rating-trends needs 24 months of ratings per category with a clear drift; helpdesk-assist needs the FAQ base widened to about 40 articles covering fees, QR, certificates, renewals, test reports, labels; document-extraction needs 5 to 6 report texts with varied formats. Include past runs with accepted, overridden and rejected dispositions so the history table is populated.
- **Dashboards after sign-in** for manufacturer, finance, programme and director should not be empty: check the My work, My approvals and Registration requests screens fed by the base tables and add rows there if they are empty for these users (Nova and Pixel own-organisation records, programme and director approval queues).

## Self-check without a database
Read each INSERT against its table definition: column count and order, NOT NULL columns, CHECK constraints, enum values, foreign keys (insert parents first), unique constraints, and any trigger or function the existing seed files call. Fewer, correct rows beat many broken ones: a single failing statement aborts that whole seed file.
